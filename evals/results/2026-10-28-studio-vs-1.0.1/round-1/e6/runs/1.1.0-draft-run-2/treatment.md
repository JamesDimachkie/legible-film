# Treatment — proposed, pending comparison

The change: a shared receipt stops being one undifferentiated total and becomes individual items that can be split. Show the actual input, user action and resulting state. This does not establish that the app recognises a receipt automatically or how taxes, tips or settlement work.

Three directions, each to be made into a short rough sketch before selection:

1. **The receipt becomes clear** (product in use): a receipt fills the screen; the real item split interaction reveals a clear allocation. Risk: the real flow may need more context than 30 seconds allows.
2. **One receipt, many choices** (relational analogy): individual lines separate like slips of paper, then connect to the real product demonstration. Risk: the metaphor could imply an unsupported gesture; label illustration and show real input and output.
3. **Beyond half** (reframe): begin on a single shared total, then reveal separate item choices through the real UI. Risk: could imply equal splitting is the only previous option; avoid that claim.

Working hypothesis: direction 1. Governing operation: a single receipt resolves into identifiable items as the actual product action proceeds. Keep the receipt and its item identity stable; change only the verified allocation state. The operation must pass a one-viewer decoding test, the competitor-swap test and muted viewing; these are pending sketches and real interface evidence, not completed results. Show a failure state if the capture supports it; do not invent one.

Turn: individual items become visible in the real workflow. Peak: the resulting split is held long enough to inspect. Ending: make the feature name, platforms and free availability clear. Density moves from a single receipt to detailed proof to a simple end card. Opening/cover proposal: a readable receipt and “Split by item”. All copy and visual states are provisional.
