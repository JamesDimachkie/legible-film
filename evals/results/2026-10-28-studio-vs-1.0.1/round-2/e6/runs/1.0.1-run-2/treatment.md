# Treatment — proposed, awaiting agreement

## Change and boundary
A shared receipt initially looks like one bill. Its individual items become visibly associated with different people. The viewer understands the new feature and where it is available. This does not prove the app's precise controls or how it handles totals, taxes, payment or scanning.

## Three concepts
1. **Product in use:** a receipt enters an authenticated product demonstration; items are assigned to people. Proof is the real operation and resulting state. Risk: actual workflow is not yet known.
2. **Relational analogy:** one paper receipt separates into item strips that settle beside different people, then resolves into a labelled illustration of the feature. Risk: the strips could imply literal UI behavior. Stop the analogy before the product view.
3. **Reframe:** “One receipt. Different items.” A single receipt transforms into individually attributed rows. Proof must still be supplied by real product capture. Risk: pretty graphics alone do not demonstrate functionality.

**Choice:** Product in use, borrowing the clear receipt-to-rows visual from the reframe. The governing operation is attribution: a single receipt remains recognizable as its individual rows become associated with different people. Keep the receipt and its item labels stable; change the visible assignment. Peak: two distinct items are attributed to two people, with both outcomes visible together. Ending: identify the feature, availability and price. Do not invent a gesture or result before capture is verified.

## Tests
- Operation: one receipt and two differently assigned rows express the idea at small scale; test on a phone with a new viewer.
- Truth: real capture is preferred; a reconstruction must be labelled illustrative and checked against actual behavior. It cannot claim unverified mechanisms.
- Decoding: a newcomer should be able to say “people can split a receipt by item in Pocketfern”; verify with a cold viewing.
- Specificity: the item-level attribution, feature name and receipt distinguish the idea from a generic budgeting app; the 50-item capacity is a limit, not a depiction of a 50-item demo.
- Muted: visible text and item-to-person relationship carry the story without audio; verify at feed size.

## Design and sound direction
Proposed art direction: a warm, tactile receipt against a quiet, high-contrast field; large type and a single accent for the current assignment, reinforced by names/shapes. Obtain actual brand tokens and mark before setting colours or typography. A purposeful, gentle instrumental bed rises with the reveal; a few soft assignment accents serve the action. No voice: legibility and message do not depend on unmuting. Music selection and its commercial licence must be approved and checked before purchase. Timing is provisional until real product behavior is captured and music is chosen.
