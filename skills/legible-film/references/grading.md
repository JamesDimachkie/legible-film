# The scorecard

Use this to help the person decide what to change. It is not release acceptance, a retention prediction or a substitute
for `check.py`, claim and rights checks, or a person's viewing and listening. An agent sees samples and reads words; it
does not experience motion or sound. Say what was observed, what was inferred and what was unavailable.

## Before scoring

Read the brief, including audience, premise, angle, platform, kind and style. Ask what change in a viewer the piece
intends, what they must notice, and what is deliberately quiet, slow, dark, static or without a call to action. If
those are unknown, say so. Do not penalise a film for omitting a device it does not need. No single shot duration,
speech rate, music level or curve is good for every piece.

The six anchors below are this rubric's **ordinal review convention**, not scientific thresholds. Intermediate
integers mean between adjacent anchors. `10` means nothing material to improve *for this brief*, not perfection in
all circumstances. A score needs a specific observation with a time and a packet reference. Quote words exactly
when the argument depends on them. A room score has only its recorded whole-cut rating and timestamp: do not invent a
scene, explanation or proof that the person watched or listened. Distinguish the person's rating, an independent
review and a self-review. If a
criterion cannot be observed, leave it waiting; use `not_scored` when it does not apply. Missing evidence is not a
zero. Apply the same anchors to versions, but do not interpret an average as comparable when coverage or basis differs.

The measurements in this release are **context and review prompts**, not numeric craft scores. A very dark shot can
be intentional; a high sample peak is not an audible clip or a true-peak measurement; a word gap need not be a
silence; a caption's sidecar duration is not its usable on-screen reading interval. Inspect the actual moment and the
brief before proposing a change. Scores remain judged or supplied by a person until a numerical mapping has passed
the relevant known-answer pairs and human-rating evaluation.

Each dimension carries a `validation` state saying what its score has been tested against (see Validation at the
end). Even a tested dimension's score is a reviewer's provisional judgment: the tests show that a gross defect moves
it, not that its numbers agree with viewers. The person's rating is recorded as their opinion, not treated as a
validation result. Do not present an average as a validated quality measure.

### Which dimension owns shared evidence

Some evidence bears on more than one question. Score each dimension only for its own question, and name the shared
cause in both notes when one defect really harms two:

- **Captions:** whether they say what is spoken (and exist where speech needs them) is `access`; whether the words on
  screen can be read, at their size, contrast and time on screen, is `text`. Wrong words with good timing lower
  `access`, not `text`; captions that leave too soon lower `text`, and `access` only if meaning is then lost.
- **Speech under music:** the balance between voice and bed is `mix`; `voice` is the recorded speech itself (noise,
  damage, consistency in the voice track); `sound_design` is the choice, placement and timing of music and effects.
- **Dependents:** the cover (`thumbnail`) is made of the film's frames, so an image or framing defect can lower it
  too; a rendition that shrinks the picture (`platform_fit`) also shrinks its text. These follow-on moves are expected;
  say which defect caused them.

## Content

### Hook (`hook`)

**Question:** Does the opening give this audience a reason to continue, while setting up the film's actual promise?

| 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No opening content. | Opening contradicts the film or cannot be understood. | Topic appears, but its relevance or question is unclear. | Promise is understandable; one delay or generic phrase weakens it. | Specific promise arrives clearly and suits the audience. | An economical opening creates a precise question or expectation that the film earns. |

Signals: first mapped word, opening samples, initial silence only if independently measured. Judge the first words and
images together; quote the promise and cite the frame. A long-form interview can establish a person before its turn;
a feed may need the subject earlier. A deliberate pause is not dead air by definition. Fix by moving a strong truthful
line or picture forward, providing necessary context, or removing an unnecessary greeting.

### Structure (`structure`)

**Question:** Can a newcomer follow the setup, development and payoff in the order presented?

| 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No coherent sequence. | Crucial premise and outcome are absent. | A major causal step is missing or the order misleads. | The sequence works, with one gap, repeated beat or weak hand-off. | Setup, development and payoff connect without avoidable repetition. | Each beat changes the viewer's understanding and prepares the next and final consequence. |

Signals: timed sections, spoken lines and picture samples; headings alone do not prove a story. Quote a setup and later
payoff and name the missing or repeated step. A montage can develop through visual relations rather than narration;
nonlinear structure is valid when the intended inference remains clear. Fix by restoring a necessary premise, changing
the order or removing a redundant beat.

### Pacing (`pacing`)

**Question:** Does information arrive at a pace this audience can absorb without unnecessary delay?

| 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No usable sequence. | Essential information is persistently absent or packed together. | A long unclear interval or a rushed passage obstructs the task. | The piece can be followed, with one slow or crowded passage. | Most beats have room to register and move on. | Reading, action and speech support the intended rhythm with no identified avoidable wait or rush. |

Signals: words and word gaps on the edit clock, section durations and sampled image changes; none measures perceived
pace or proves a silence. Cite the passage and the information competing there. Tutorials may need holds; a meditative
film can be intentionally slow. Fix by removing a redundant beat, extending a reading interval, or separating competing
tasks. A person must check rhythm at speed.

### Emotion (`emotion`)

**Question:** Does the piece support its declared feeling or emotional turn?

| 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No identifiable intended feeling or material. | The observed material directly defeats the declared feeling. | The intended turn is named but has no clear preparation. | One passage plausibly supports the feeling; the turn is uneven. | Observable choices consistently prepare and develop the intended turn. | The evidence and payoff make a distinctive emotional turn plausible for the intended audience. |

Signals: the brief, quoted words, picture samples and the measured sound envelope only as context. Cite the intention
and the specific turn. A critic cannot infer how viewers felt from stills; keep the score low confidence and prefer the
person's response. A restrained documentary and a playful ad need different devices. Fix the story, detail or timing
that prepares the feeling rather than automatically adding music.

### Ending and next step (`cta`)

**Question:** Does the ending complete the film's thought and, when requested, make the next step clear?

| 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The cut ends before any ending. | The final thought is interrupted or contradicts the setup. | The ending exists but its consequence or required action is unclear. | The ending is intelligible with one weak or rushed element. | The payoff and any requested action are clear and have room. | The ending resolves or deliberately reframes the promise, and any action is natural and specific. |

Signals: final words, final section and text frames; a section named CTA is not proof of a usable ending. Quote the
final thought and cite the picture. A loop, poem or story may need a landing rather than an invitation. Fix a cut-off
sentence, restore the consequence, or hold the actual next step long enough for the intended viewer.

## Picture

### Cover (`thumbnail`)

**Question:** Does a candidate frame communicate the subject and useful promise at the intended viewing size?

| 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No usable candidate frame. | The subject is absent or essential information is obscured. | A subject is present but unclear at viewing size. | The subject is recognisable with one competing or unreadable element. | A clear, distinctive frame accurately represents the film. | Subject and promise are immediately legible, distinctive and faithful to the film at the actual placement size. |

Signals: sampled candidate frames, luma spread and spatial detail. Optional OpenCV uses its bundled frontal-face cascade
on reduced samples; detections can be wrong and absence of a detection does not mean absence of a face. These signals do
not reliably read text or predict clicks. Quote visible text as an observation and cite the frame; verify the exact full-size frame before export.
Different platforms crop and display covers differently; check the actual placement. Fix by selecting another moment,
composing an honest cover or simplifying the subject. The packet's 168-by-94-pixel landscape inspection is a local
review convention, not a platform requirement; portrait tiles retain aspect ratio.

### Cut rhythm (`visual_pacing`)

**Question:** Do picture changes support the content and the intended timing reference?

| 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No picture to evaluate. | Repeated unintended breaks obscure the action. | A major hold or change obscures an important beat. | Picture supports the piece, with one awkward transition or hold. | Changes consistently support the content and declared clock. | Every observed transition has a legible purpose and the overall rhythm supports the piece. |

Signals: compiled picture-line boundaries, FFmpeg `scdet` candidate changes, `siti` spatial and temporal information,
and `freezedetect` events. Detected changes are candidates: motion or flashes may trigger them, and subtle cuts may be
missed. A freeze can be intentional. None establishes perceived rhythm. Cite the boundary and what the viewer should follow. A long take may be correct for an interview, while a
montage can use dense cuts; neither has a universal cuts-per-minute target. Fix the transition's cause, not the average
shot length. Inspect at normal speed.

### Text and captions (`text`)

**Question:** Can the words on screen be read, at their size, contrast and placement, in the time they are shown?

| 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Required words are absent. | Essential meaning is wrong or unreadable throughout. | An important word, qualifier or interval cannot be read. | Most text is legible; one cue is crowded, obscured or inaccurate. | Text, timing and hierarchy work in sampled moments. | The person confirms accurate text and readable windows throughout the delivered rendition. |

Signals: `detail.png` (exact frames at up to 640 px wide, where burned captions and titles are readable), the
opening sheet, and sidecar cue start, end and words per minute when a sidecar exists. Caption accuracy against speech
is scored under Accessibility. BBC subtitle guidance describes roughly 160-180 words per minute
for many genres, with editorial adjustments; this is a review prompt, not a pass/fail score or proof for burned text.
See [BBC subtitle guidance](https://www.bbc.co.uk/accessibility/forproducts/guides/subtitles/) and
[BBC research on subtitle speed](https://downloads.bbc.co.uk/rd/pubs/whp/whp-pdf-files/WHP306.pdf). Cite and quote the
affected cue, and inspect rendered frames and the fully readable interval. Children's content, complex language,
simultaneous action, and platform overlays change the needed window. Fix an inaccurate word, extend the usable hold,
reduce clutter or reposition the caption. Verify on the actual platform preview when overlays matter.

### Lighting and image (`image`)

**Question:** Does image treatment preserve the details the piece needs and remain consistent where intended?

| 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No image. | Essential subjects are consistently invisible or irretrievably clipped. | A material exposure, colour, focus or noise defect hides relevant detail. | The picture works with one visible inconsistency or defect. | The sampled picture retains needed detail and the treatment is consistent. | The intended image treatment supports the subject, with no material defect found in a complete human review. |

Signals: per-frame FFmpeg `signalstats` low, average and high luma, saturation, hue, temporal outliers and broadcast-range
share; `blurdetect`, `blockdetect` and per-candidate-shot luma and saturation means. The code values use FFmpeg's
negotiated filter format; source range, bit depth, intentional lighting and colour management matter. Broadcast-range
share is not a general clipping verdict, especially for full-range material. These values do not establish colour
accuracy, perceptual sharpness, noise or general image quality. Cite a frame and the detail lost. A silhouette, fade or
low-key scene can be deliberate. Fix source exposure, colour or noise only after checking a full-size frame and the
intended look; avoid crushing a deliberate dark scene to satisfy a number.

### Framing (`composition`)

**Question:** Can the viewer find and follow the intended subject without an unintended crop or distraction?

| 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No picture. | Required subject is absent. | An important subject is cut off or hidden in a key passage. | Framing works with one competing or cramped moment. | The subject and its relevant action remain clear in sampled moments. | Framing guides attention and preserves all needed context in the delivered rendition. |

Signals: overview, cover and opening sheets, optional approximate frontal-face detections, and per-frame `cropdetect`
black-border candidates. A dark scene or intentional letterboxing can resemble a border. The face detector can miss faces
and does not detect the intended subject. Cite the subject,
its frame and the missing context. A centred close-up, off-centre portrait and deliberately obstructed shot can all be
appropriate. Fix the crop path, camera selection or overlay; check every rendition at its actual size.

## Sound

### Voice (`voice`)

**Question:** Is speech clear, undamaged and appropriate for its role?

| 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Required speech is absent. | The intended speech cannot be understood. | Noise, masking or damage obscures a substantial phrase. | Speech is understandable with one distracting defect. | The person hears clear, consistent speech. | The person hears clear, expressive speech that remains natural in the mix. |

Signals: `sound.png`, per-channel FFmpeg `astats` peak, RMS and DC offset, `ebur128` momentary loudness on the mix
and `speech_vs_pause`: the median momentary loudness inside transcribed phrases against the median inside pauses
between them. A small difference means something (noise, room, music) fills the pauses; it does not say what, and it
needs a real transcript (a sparse transcript gives none). A local transcript of a file's own audio that mishears
words the brief or captions make clear is evidence of masking or damage worth listening for. A high sample peak is a
prompt to inspect; it does not prove clipping. These are not a speech detector, intelligibility, a speech
noise floor, true peak or a voice-quality model. Cite exact speech, the interval and the actual listening or measured defect. An agent that
cannot hear cannot assert how speech sounds; leave unsupported quality unscored. Unvoiced films are not failures.
Fix the source, edit, dialogue cleanup or masking cause; avoid destroying speech in pursuit of a noise number.

### Music and effects (`sound_design`)

**Question:** Do sound choices support the picture and intended feeling without distracting?

| 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Required sound is absent. | Sound defeats the intended action or obscures required speech. | A sustained cue distracts from the intended task. | The sound supports the piece with one distracting choice. | The person hears purposeful sounds, including deliberate silence. | Sound and silence deepen the intended moments without an identified distraction. |

Signals: audio stream presence, `sound.png` and the mixed level profile; music that stops dead before the picture
ends is visible there. They cannot identify music, effects, their roles, onset intent or taste: in testing, a
replaced music bed and added chimes did not show in the packet at all. Cite the picture event, cue and listener; use cue-onset checks on a separate stem where there is an explicit sync
contract. A silent piece is not automatically weak. Fix the specific cue's role, position or level, and compare at similar
perceived loudness with a person listening.

### Mix (`mix`)

**Question:** Can the intended sound elements be heard together cleanly and consistently?

| 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Required audio is missing. | A required element is inaudible or the mix is severely damaged. | Masking or a level jump disrupts an important passage. | The mix works with one audible imbalance or defect. | The person hears a clean, balanced mix for the piece. | The person hears controlled, purposeful dynamics across the whole delivered mix. |

Signals: `sound.png` (a hard stop, a level step or a raised floor is visible there), momentary loudness,
`speech_vs_pause`, per-channel peak and DC offset, and, only on stereo, `aphasemeter` phase correlation.
Negative correlation can locate a possible mono compatibility problem, but the person should compare the actual mono
fold-down; unrelated stereo material and silence need context. These signals do not establish voice-to-music separation
or true peak; use stems and `qc_file.py` when relevant. Quote the affected line and identify the listened interval. The
brief or distributor sets delivery targets; there is no universal loudness target for all creative work. Fix masking,
gain automation or a damaged source, then listen to the delivered encode.

The card prompts inspection above a sample peak of -0.1 dBFS or below a stereo phase correlation of -0.8; it excludes
windows below a -60 dBFS sample peak from the latter prompt. These are local screening conventions, not proven audibility
or clipping thresholds. Per-frame metadata retains the measurements, including quiet windows, for independent review.

## Delivery

### Platform fit (`platform_fit`)

**Question:** Does the actual rendition support the declared placement and its viewer task?

| 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No usable rendition. | A required format or essential muted message is absent. | Important content is cropped or unusable in the stated placement. | The rendition works with one placement-specific weakness. | Shape and sampled content suit the stated placement. | The person confirms the message and layout in the actual placement and requested renditions. |

Signals: encoded shape and duration, first frame, captions and declared platform; these do not establish current platform
limits, safe-zone clearance or muted comprehension. Cite the requirement and the affected frame. Platform overlays and
policies change; verify the current official guide, for example [YouTube upload guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171).
A site loop and a vertical feed have different tasks. Fix framing, the reading window or the rendition, not a generic
duration target.

### Accessibility (`access`)

**Question:** Do the alternatives carry the necessary meaning (captions that match the speech), is the film free of
harmful flashing, and which checks remain open?

| 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Required accessible information is absent. | A major required alternative or safety check fails. | Essential speech or visual meaning is inaccessible in a substantial passage. | Alternatives exist but one material issue or check remains unresolved. | Available checks and sampled alternatives support access, with named limits. | The person confirms the required alternatives and delivery-specific accessibility checks. |

Signals: `checks.json` states and sidecar timing; `skip`, `you` and `look` are not passes. Compare the captions read
in `detail.png` with `transcript.md` (both can be wrong; a mismatch is a reason to listen). The card prompts possible
flashing when six or more large whole-frame brightness changes fall within one second; that screen misses local and
red flashes and is not a photosensitivity test. Cite the check, standard and
affected interval; a draft transcript cannot establish caption accuracy. For applicable web content,
[WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) requires captions for prerecorded synchronized audio except its stated
exception, and limits flashing under its stated thresholds. It also gives text contrast criteria with exceptions. Those
are accessibility criteria, not a substitute for a specialist flash test or a quality score. The audience, medium and
applicable standard decide what else is needed. Fix missing or inaccurate captions, inaccessible visual information,
contrast or the actual flash; rerun the relevant check and ask a person to verify.

## Judging and reading the card

Before rendering, `grade.py paper PROJECT` compiles the edit into `grade/paper.json` and `grade/paper.md` and creates
a `paper-judged-template-<id>.json`. Read the paper edit's stated direction (`audience`, `takeaway`, `story`, printed
at the top of `paper.md`), the brief and the rubric; judge whether the opening creates an appropriate expectation,
the sequence develops it, and the ending completes or deliberately reframes it. Then fill a copy of that template
with the reviewer
name, whether they are independent, and any supported hook, structure, pacing or cta ratings: each is an object in
`dimensions` whose `id` names it (`{"id": "hook", "score": 7, ...}`). A rating has integer
`score`, `confidence`, and at least one `evidence` item with estimated `t`, `end`, an exact transcript `quote`, and a
`note` explaining it. Optionally add a `fix` with text, paper line and estimated gain. Run
`grade.py paper PROJECT --judged filled.json` to validate the compiled-edit identity, quote and timing and record the
review. Include the same judge provenance described below. Keep unobservable dimensions waiting; an estimated transcript
interval is not rendered sound or playback. The paper identity follows the compiled lines, their order, words,
titles, settings and the brief, rather than notes in `film.md` or their physical line numbers. A matching review
survives note edits; a changed edit or brief leaves it in `grade/paper-history/` and the command reports that it no
longer matches. Returning to that exact edit restores its recorded assessments. Pre-versioned reviews migrate only
when their saved paper, original rubric and brief can be verified; otherwise they remain in history as stale.
Section line numbers are the physical `film.md` lines used by `cut.py check`.

`grade.py hooks PROJECT [--alias ALIAS]` ranks the top five punctuated candidates using length, a simple
self-containment cue and mapped quiet after each sentence; `--all` shows all. It prints them and writes `grade/hooks.json` and
`grade/hooks.md`. These cues do not judge story or predict retention: read the candidates and listen to the joins.
`grade.py paper --help`, `hooks --help` and `review --help` show their own arguments and outputs; paper help
includes the judged JSON shape.

Run `grade.py PROJECT` or `grade.py --file FILE`; it writes measurements, an overview, candidate covers, a dense opening,
readable detail frames (`detail.png`), a loudness picture (`sound.png`), the available strips, the edit-timed
transcript, this rubric and a local `judged.json` template. A loose file's card is `NAME.scorecard.json` and
`NAME.scorecard.html` beside it, with its packet in `NAME.grade/`, so several files in one folder keep separate cards.

A loose file has no paper edit, so its words come from one of three places, and `transcript.md` and the card's
`transcript` say which: `--words FILE` imports an existing transcript (word JSON, SRT or WebVTT on the file's clock;
cue files give evenly spread, estimated word times); otherwise, when faster-whisper and its small model are installed,
the file's own audio is transcribed locally (`transcribe.py`, nothing uploaded, cached in `NAME.grade/`); otherwise
the card says plainly that there is no transcript and how to get one (`--no-transcribe` turns local transcription off).
Without words, hook, structure, pacing and ending rest on pictures and sound measurements alone; say so in any score.
A transcript with only a few words over a whole film is marked `sparse`: transcribers can invent a word or two over
music, so do not treat it as speech. It streams per-frame
FFmpeg measurements into `grade/frames.jsonl.gz`, keyed to the file and analysis configuration; a matching complete
cache is reused. Its footer and checksum reject incomplete or changed cache content. Each line is a JSON object with a
type: header, video, audio, sample or footer. Frame records carry FFmpeg presentation timestamps and the original filter
metadata names; no frame timing is reconstructed from a nominal frame rate. The packet also contains a hard link or
copy of this sidecar. `scopes/index.json` maps waveform and vectorscope pictures to the representative frame and time
interval for each automatically detected candidate shot; long cuts cap the scope count and disclose that sampling.
[QCTools filter descriptions](https://bavc.github.io/qctools/filter_descriptions.html) inspired these diagnostics;
their meaning is tied to the FFmpeg filters, source range and the shot, not to a universal quality threshold.
Open `packet.json` for
sampling limits and missing artifacts. Another reviewer should receive this packet when available. Never upload a private
packet without the person's permission. A reviewer fills a copy of the template with scores and cited evidence, then
`grade.py merge PROJECT filled.json` (or `grade.py merge filled.json --file FILE`) validates identity, times,
references and transcript quotes before writing the card.
The validation establishes that a citation exists; it does not prove the reviewer's interpretation.

Each rendered card's `media_sha256` is the SHA-256 of the actual graded file. The packet, submission and file must
match before a review is merged. A review submission also needs this structured, reviewer-reported provenance:

```json
{"judge_provenance": {"model_family": "example-family", "model": "example-model",
                      "same_family_as_cut_maker": false}}
```

Use a boolean when the maker's family is known; use JSON `null` when it is unknown. Missing model or family, or a
missing or invalid relationship, prevents a merge. The selected judged dimension and each preserved judged assessment
carry these fields. They are the reviewer's declaration, not independently verified identity. An unknown relationship
reduces confidence to low. Old assessments without recorded provenance retain null fields and low confidence; do not
infer a model from a display name. Person scores are attributed to the person and do not have model provenance.

For each score, name who judged, whether they were independent, the observation, the uncertainty and a possible fix.
A person can provide their own score in the Screening Room. The room alone writes project-root `scores.json`:

```json
{"version": 1, "scores": {"v003": {"hook": {"score": 7, "by": "person", "at": "2026-10-29T12:00:00Z"}}}}
```

Run `grade.py scores PROJECT --version v003` after the room changes that file. This checks the video and packet identity,
updates both scorecard formats without a video decode, and never writes `scores.json`. The scorecard retains the
reviewer's assessments; the person's score is the selected basis for that dimension and its overall contribution. Its
whole-cut evidence has `t: null` because the room's format supplies no timecode or explanation. Removing that rating
restores the reviewer's score or the unscored state. An attributed reviewer fix can remain visible alongside a person's
score. A score supplied by a person is not proof of viewing or listening. The room should surface refresh errors;
status 1 means the card still has unscored dimensions, while status 2 is an error.

Self-review is low confidence. Stills and transcripts are not a normal-speed screening; voice and sound taste cannot be
heard by an agent limited to this packet. A person's viewing and listening outrank these inferences. Ranked gains are
reviewer estimates, not measured improvements. Compare matching dimensions and evidence across parent versions; the
partial average can change merely because coverage changes. Safety, factual accuracy, rights and delivery failures stay
separate from an attractive average.

## Validation

Each dimension's `validation` on the card says what its score has been tested against. Method (2026-10-30): one
interview cut with burned captions and one b-roll montage over music were made from public-domain and CC BY footage;
for each dimension one copy changed only that thing, all copies in the same encoding generation. One reviewer from a
different model family than the grader's builder scored all fifteen dimensions on every reference/degraded pair from
the packets alone, labelled A and B in random order, with the key sealed until all scores were recorded. That reviewer
also made the degradations, so the test was blind to labels but not to what kinds of change existed. A dimension
passes on a cut when its score drops by at least 3 and no other dimension moves by more than 2. `passed_degradation_test`
means it passed on both cuts (voice: the interview only, the montage has no speech); `partly_passed` means one of
two; `unvalidated` means neither. Repeat judging of 8 pairs agreed within one point on every score, but the same
reviewer recognised the packets, so that is a floor on its spread, not a measurement of it.

| Dimension | State | Tested against | Not tested |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | passed_degradation_test | a greeting or bland shot before the promise: drop 4 and 3 | subtle openings, retention |
| Structure | passed_degradation_test | a shuffled middle: drop 3 and 4 | subtle order problems |
| Pacing | partly_passed | dead air after each sentence: drop 3; slowed montage shots: drop 2 | perceived pace at speed |
| Emotion | unvalidated | a contradicting music bed: drop 0 and 1 | anything heard; viewer feeling |
| Ending | passed_degradation_test | an ending cut mid-thought: drop 6 and 5 | weak but complete endings |
| Cover | unvalidated | a blurred first second: drop 2 and 2 | clicks, placement crops |
| Cut rhythm | partly_passed | jump-cut toggling: drop 4; erratic montage cuts: drop 4, pacing moved 3 | rhythm at speed, beat sync |
| Text | partly_passed | faint title: drop 5; captions shown half their time: drop 5, access moved 3 | text in other languages |
| Image | passed_degradation_test | two stops under: drop 4 and 4 (cover followed by 2) | subtle grading, noise, focus |
| Framing | partly_passed | subjects cropped out: drop 4 and 4 (cover followed by 2 and 3) | subtle headroom, shake |
| Voice | unvalidated | noise under the voice: drop 2, mix moved 2 | anything heard |
| Music and effects | partly_passed | music stopping 12 s early: drop 3; added chimes: drop 0 | taste, fit, effects |
| Mix | partly_passed | an 8 dB step with overs: drop 4; music over the voice: drop 4, voice and music moved 3 | true peak, platform targets |
| Platform fit | unvalidated | a windowboxed vertical rendition: drop 5 and 5, text followed by 3 | current platform rules |
| Accessibility | partly_passed | a full-frame strobe: drop 6; wrong caption words: drop 5, text moved 5 | specialist flash testing |

The rule about other dimensions is strict on purpose. Several failures are one defect harming two questions (wrong
captions under both text and access; cropped faces under both framing and cover); the ownership notes above now say
which dimension owns which evidence, but they were written after the test and have not been retested blind.

Human ratings. On a random 500 of KoNViD-1k's in-the-wild videos (viewer quality scores), the Spearman rank correlation
of the per-film median FFmpeg `blurdetect` value with the score was -0.51 (95% bootstrap interval -0.57 to -0.44);
`blockdetect` -0.35, spatial information +0.35, average luma +0.35, saturation, broadcast-range share and temporal
information near 0. These are moderate at best and do not justify a numeric image score; they stay context. On
NISQA's rated speech (phone and VoIP recordings, 24 to 32 votes per file), sample peak, DC offset and loudness did not
track overall quality once subsets were pooled; `speech_vs_pause` tracked listeners' noisiness ratings (Spearman 0.69,
interval 0.61 to 0.75, 215 files with enough pauses) and overall quality more weakly (0.47). Most of those recordings
with speech under 15 LU above the pauses were rated noticeably noisy, so the card turns that into a prompt to listen,
not a score: a film's music bed also fills pauses on purpose. No voice score is mapped from it.
