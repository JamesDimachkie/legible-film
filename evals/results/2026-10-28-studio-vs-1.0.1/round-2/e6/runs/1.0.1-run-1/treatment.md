# Treatment — proposed, not yet approved

## Change and boundary
A shared receipt begins as one undivided object. The viewer sees individual items assigned to different people and understands the new split-by-item feature. The example is illustrative until the actual workflow is verified; it does not establish receipt capture, exact interface behavior or how payments, tax and shared items work.

## Three concepts
1. **Product in use:** A receipt, then a clear action and result in the actual interface. Strongest proof if a real, synthetic-data capture can be supplied.
2. **Relational analogy:** Receipt rows move toward their respective people, like place cards at a table. The relation explains assignment; it must not imply an unsupported automation.
3. **Reframe:** “One receipt. Different orders.” shifts the question from dividing the total to assigning items. It gives context quickly, but words alone do not demonstrate the feature.

**Chosen: product in use**, borrowing the spatial clarity of the analogy and the opening line of the reframe. Governing operation: one receipt resolves into individual item assignments; the same receipt and people remain visible, while the assignment state changes. The analogy stops at assigning items.

## Tests
- **Operation:** One row, one person and a visible assigned state is the smallest readable action; repeat with a second row to show the distinction.
- **Truth:** Real behavior and states need verification. A designed stand-in must say “Illustrative example” and must not present invented screens as a recording.
- **Decoding:** A new viewer should be able to say “people can split a receipt by item in Pocketfern”; verify with a first-time viewer.
- **Specificity:** Item-level assignment, the feature name and Pocketfern identify this announcement; a generic total split cannot substitute for the demonstration.
- **Muted:** Copy, visible action and result carry the story; music adds pace but no required information.

## Structure
Opening: one receipt, different orders. Turn: introduce split by item. Peak: the second item visibly lands with a different person. Ending: name the product and feature, availability, capacity and free access with enough reading time. The visual thread is the receipt and its rows. Real UI and exact action are provisional until supplied and checked.
