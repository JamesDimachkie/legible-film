# Sound plan

The 20-second demo is silent: it has one video stream and no audio. The image is minimal—a light panel and blue control on a dark background, followed by a green line that grows near the end. The sound should make those changes feel intentional without giving a simple interface more drama than it needs.

- **Music:** Use a restrained instrumental electronic bed: warm sustained synth, a soft pulse, and a little forward motion. No vocals or prominent melody. Begin quietly, develop slightly as the interaction progresses, and resolve cleanly at the end. Avoid a heavy beat or a generic cinematic rise.
- **0–4 seconds:** Fade in the bed gently as the panel appears. Leave space for the visual to establish itself.
- **Around 5–10 seconds:** Add a soft, precise interface tick when the blue control appears or is activated. If the small white marker represents a cursor, use one subdued click at the actual activation, not for every movement.
- **Around 12–16 seconds:** Let the music open up subtly as the green line begins and extends. A quiet, smooth tonal swell can follow its growth; avoid repeated beeps.
- **16–20 seconds:** Hold the musical texture while the completed line remains visible, then give it a short, natural resolution that finishes with the picture.

These timings are approximate, based on sampled frames. Before placing cues, inspect the video frame by frame to identify the actual control activation and the line's start and end; align sounds to those events rather than to the rounded times above.

For the eventual mix, keep effects clearly audible but subordinate to the music, use short fades to avoid clicks, and check on headphones and small speakers for harshness, clipping, and a clean ending. Export a separate video with the original picture preserved and a stereo audio track. Use properly licensed or original sounds; any paid generation or licensing requires your approval first.
