# Preferences: Pocketfern launch film plans

I read REQUEST.md and every file in the four reply folders, and nothing else. These are plans, so no film, sketch or sound exists to watch or hear. I did not open the three web pages cited in pair 1 Q's study.md. Judgments about ideas, copy and design are my opinions.

## Pair 1

| Criterion | Preference | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| a. Creative thinking | no clear difference | P has the sharper picture and a real audience insight: "One receipt. Different orders." and "Show two different items assigned to two people, one action at a time." Q explores more before committing ("All three are provisional until you see rough moving sketches"), but its recommended film is only "Actual app capture; show real item-level interaction." |
| b. Leading a newcomer | Q | Q gives a route in plain words: "Make three short rough sketches with temporary sound. Choose the story and any idea worth keeping from the others," then stills, then "a rough full-length preview to decide the look and pacing." P's decisions are "Approve or revise the proposed story and brief," then purchases, then posting; no draft is promised. P's 30-second table is the more tangible of the two. |
| c. Money and rights | Q | Q's budget.csv gives every line "max_cost_usd_including_tax", "rights_required" and a "fallback", and the reply asks "whether the post may be boosted, the countries and intended length of use, and whether you may want shorter or translated versions." P says "commercial licences must be checked before any purchase" but assumes organic X use, never asks about use, and allocates the whole cap ("$100 for effects, $200 for optional stock or generation"). |
| d. Craft on screen | P | P decides the picture and timing: "framed tightly enough to read in a phone feed," "roughly 384 px feed width," "two subtle assignment accents," a "resolved musical tail." Q's core is "Actual app capture" and "Stable hold on evidence," and its look line is only "restrained product-led typography." |
| e. Honesty | no clear difference | Both stay inside the given facts and say what is unseen. P: "those details are not invented in the plan." Q: "I cannot watch motion at normal speed or hear the mix," with claims marked "user supplied; verification pending." Q's study quotes outside pages as "makers report," which I did not check. |

**Overall: Q. Confidence: medium.**

**Biggest weakness, P:** It runs in a straight line from brief approval to purchases to release. A first-timer gets no sketches, stills or rough cut to react to, and the plan assumes organic X use without asking about boosting, countries or term, so the music licence scope is never defined.

**Biggest weakness, Q:** The middle half of the film is left to a recording that does not exist yet ("Actual app capture," "Actual app result, held legibly"), so the person cannot picture what they will get. The unwatched reference study of three agency portfolio pages adds no idea the plan uses.

What most separated these was how each leads someone who has never made a video. Q sets out the drafts and decisions in order, ties each budget line to rights and a fallback, and asks how the post will be used before music is bought. P is tighter and shows the 30 seconds more concretely, but it goes from brief approval straight to purchases and release. The ideas and honesty were close, so the route and the licensing questions decided it.

**Overall: P. Confidence: medium.**

**Biggest weakness, P:** The sequence and look are thin. "Show the actual selection and allocation actions" and "Brand end card" leave type, colour, text holds and sound beats undecided, so the person cannot yet picture the film, and the plan is one dense page whose checks (BT.709, true peak) they cannot use.

**Biggest weakness, Q:** It plans one approval of a technical brief and treatment, then production. It promises no sketches, stills or rough cut, so a first-timer has nothing to steer before the finished file.

What most separated these was the route and the questions put to the person. P promises moving sketches, then look stills, then a timed rough, tells the person to respond in ordinary language, and asks about the old workflow and the intended use. Q's treatment is better specified on screen, with real art direction and a clean map from text to facts, but it plans a single approval and its message leans on specifications. Q has the better paper treatment; P is the plan a first-timer can steer.
