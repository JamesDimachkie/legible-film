# Pocketfern launch film: plan

Status: planning only. Nothing purchased, generated, uploaded, rendered or published. Creative choices are proposals; the brief and story are not yet approved.

## The film
A 30-second, 1920 × 1080, 30 fps, 16:9 H.264 film for X, with music and restrained effects; readable with sound off. One master and a cover frame. No separate captions file is planned unless voice is chosen. Exact X upload requirements will be verified before export. The audience is people sharing expenses, including existing Pocketfern users and people unfamiliar with it. The intended change: understand that a shared receipt can be split by individual item in Pocketfern and that the feature is free.

Recommended direction: **The receipt finds its people.** A receipt is the visual thread. Show a real, verified Pocketfern interaction assigning items, then hold the resulting state long enough to understand it. Open with the problem as a visual question, reveal the action, end with the launch facts. The precise gestures, screens, assignment logic and resulting state must come from an actual product recording; do not invent them. Until provided, any rough interface is labelled illustration, never evidence.

## Three directions to compare
1. **Product in use:** One shared receipt; a person assigns individual items in the real app. Direct and demonstrable; its risk is small interface text. Rough sketch: receipt arrives → an item is assigned → the resulting state holds.
2. **Receipt finds its people:** Receipt rows travel visually toward their owners, then resolve into the actual product demonstration. Stronger visual thread; risk of implying automatic assignment. Clearly depict the user's real action and keep the metaphor separate from proof. Rough sketch: one receipt → rows separate → real app confirms the action.
3. **A different question:** Start with “Who had what?” and replace the whole-bill question with item-level choices in the real product. Clear hook, but more dependent on copy and typography. Rough sketch: question → individual items → verified result.

All three pass a provisional operation test in words: a receipt changes from a shared list to item-level allocation. Decoding, specificity and truth require actual sketches and product evidence; none is yet a passed viewer test. Recommend direction 2, retaining direction 1's clear proof and direction 3's opening question. The choice is provisional until rough moving sketches can be watched.

## Proposed timeline
| Time | Picture and text | Sound and purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 s | A receipt and the question “Who had what?” | A restrained opening; immediate hook readable muted. |
| 4–8 s | Introduce “Split by item” and Pocketfern; receipt rows separate as an explicitly illustrative transition. | Music begins to build; introduce the feature. |
| 8–19 s | Real product capture: show one or more items being assigned and hold the verified result. Use only real states; crop and enlarge for phone viewing. | Sparse tactile accents tied to actual actions; the demonstration is the peak. |
| 19–25 s | “Up to 50 items per receipt” and “Free for all users” in separate readable holds. | Music supports the reading, with no busy effects. |
| 25–30 s | “Split by item is here” / “iOS & Android” / Pocketfern identity. | Resolve gently; leave the final identity on screen. |

All timing is provisional until a viewing-size animatic proves it readable. “Launched today” is a dated claim; use “is here” in the film and verify launch timing again before release. No unsupported claim about speed, automation, accuracy, receipt scanning, payment, availability by country, or a specific workflow is implied. This demonstration does not establish any of those things.

## Picture and sound
Use approved brand colours, type and logo once supplied. Until then, use clearly temporary neutral design. The camera follows the receipt; interface evidence stays stable and unobscured. Use real product capture or a verified reconstruction only after matching actual behavior. No generative model should invent the interface. Screen text is the primary explanation, so the film works muted. Compare two loudness-matched sound treatments against the same animatic: warm tactile music with sparse effects, and a lighter music-led option. Voice is optional and not required to understand the film; decide only after seeing the animatic. Do not buy a track or voice until its intended use and exact licence are clear.

## Production and review
1. Obtain real product material and confirm facts and rights. Develop the three short moving concept sketches with temporary sound, then choose one.
2. Make opening, proof, densest text and ending styleframes at full size and viewing size. Show what is temporary. Build a timed animatic with those states and temporary sound; review it muted and sounded.
3. Compare the sound directions and licensed candidates, then request approval for each specific purchase or generation batch. Lock picture after a muted viewing; finish the mix after picture lock.
4. Export and inspect the actual delivery: full decode, format and colour tags; contrast; flash, blank and motion scans; text and claims; audio peak after lossy re-encode, cue and sync checks. Inspect all flags and transition sheets. Record assets, permissions, settings and hashes.
5. A named person watches the final file at normal speed, first muted at typical viewing size, then with sound, and logs problems. Test with an intended viewer if available: can they identify the feature, explain what they saw, identify Pocketfern, understand the item limit and price, and know the next action? Repair the weakest moment and repeat. The final release remains subject to explicit approval.
