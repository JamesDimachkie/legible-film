1. Preserve the approved v5 composition, exported film, cue list and mastered mix as a read-only baseline, with hashes. Record the approved duration, frame rate, format, claims, design, timing and sound, and identify the ending's intended job.
2. Inspect the ending and its lead-in using dense frame sheets, timing and reading windows, the composition's timing source, cue list and decoded audio measurements. Treat “rushed” as a symptom: test whether the cause is a late landing, insufficient readable hold, an early exit, competing motion, or the sound's cadence. Do not assume which without the files.
3. Make the smallest ending-local change supported by that evidence. Preserve the approved entry, content, visual design and earlier sequence. If the note requires a material design change, prepare a short motion proposal for review before committing to that direction.
4. Keep picture and sound on their shared clock. If a picture-only adjustment leaves every cue unchanged, re-export the cue list and require a byte-for-byte match before reusing the master; verify decoded audio hashes where sound should be unchanged. If a hold shifts cues or changes duration, update the timing source and cue export together and conform the mix only as necessary, preserving the approved sound elsewhere. Check any animation or sound spanning the hold explicitly. Do not truncate the picture to fit the mix.
5. Render a short ending window first, then the full version from a frozen source copy, using the separate master for muxing. Compare v5 and the revision, accounting explicitly for any intended added duration and shifted tail; inspect all differences and every use of any shared code changed.
6. Check the delivered file's decode, specs and colour, flash, blank frames, motion, transitions, text and claims, plus audio peaks, cue onsets and sync where applicable. Record flags, hashes, tool versions, render settings and the exact changed interval. A named person must watch at normal speed and listen to the revised file; until then it is unwatched and unheard. Provide a playable local review copy and report; sending or publishing requires approval.
