# Case library

Published short films, ads and title sequences, indexed by the problem they solve. Use a case to see how a problem
was solved; never copy its look, device or palette, and compare your own stills with it to make sure you have not.
Credits and descriptions were checked against public sources on 2026-10-27; watch a piece yourself before citing a
detail of it.

## Story and picture

| Problem | Case | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Proving a capability so the viewer believes it | Volvo Trucks, "The Epic Split" (2013; Forsman & Bodenfors; dir. Andreas Nilsson) | Jean-Claude Van Damme does the splits between two reversing trucks to demonstrate the stability and precision of Volvo Dynamic Steering; shot live in one take, with a hidden safety harness removed in post. The stunt would visibly fail if the product did. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Epic_Split) |
| A chain of visible causes | Honda, "Cog" (2003; Wieden+Kennedy London; dir. Antoine Bardou-Jacquet) | A chain reaction of real car parts, each setting off the next, largely without computer graphics. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cog_(advertisement)) |
| One rule of the world changes | Apple, AirPods "Bounce" (2019; TBWA\Media Arts Lab; dir. Oscar Hudson) | A commute in which the city becomes a trampoline; the film explores the consequences of one changed rule. [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/creative-works/tbwa-media-arts-lab-apple-bounce) |
| Telling a human story through use of the product | Google, "Parisian Love" (2009 Search Stories film; aired during Super Bowl XLIV, 2010) | A love story told through one person's searches. |
| A belief behind the product | Honda, "Grrr" (2004; Wieden+Kennedy London; dirs. Adam Foulkes and Alan Smith) | Reportedly grew from a Honda engine designer's dislike of diesel engines ("Hate something, change something"). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grrr_(advertisement)) |
| A number the viewer must understand | Ed Hawkins, "climate spiral" (2016) | Monthly temperature anomalies drawn on fixed axes, month as angle and anomaly as radius, with colour encoding time. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_spiral) |
| Live data as short animations | IBM, "Datagrams" (2013; IBM with Ogilvy & Mather; animation by Buck) | Live match data from the US Open turned into short animations for each match. [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/live-from-the-us-open-ibm-datagrams-brings-the-action-from-the-courts-immediately-to-instagram-222669621.html) |
| A values statement in few words | Apple, "Intention" (2013, Designed by Apple in California; TBWA\Media Arts Lab) | Short sentences given simple visual relationships. |
| One silhouette through many materials | Nike, "Air Max '17: The Art of Negative Space" (ManvsMachine) | The negative-space outline of the shoe recurs through scenes of different materials; the sound re-pitches footsteps into melody and rhythm. [Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/work/go-lighter-go-longer-manvsmachines-animation-nike-air-max/) |
| Delaying recognition of a title | "Alien" (1979; R/Greenberg Associates, Richard Greenberg) | The title's letters assemble gradually, so viewers are not ready to read them as a word. [Art of the Title](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/alien) |
| Motion that belongs to its period | "Stranger Things" (2016; Imaginary Forces, Michelle Dougherty) | An early "locking in" letter motion felt rigid and snappy, and the creators did not respond to it because it was not a familiar 1980s motion; the final type drifts and slowly assembles. [Art of the Title](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/stranger-things) |
| Unsettling the viewer on purpose | "Severance" main titles (2022; Oliver Latta / Extraweg; typography Teddy Blanks; music Theodore Shapiro) | A title sequence built to disorient rather than reassure. [It's Nice That](https://itsnicethat.com/articles/oliver-latta-severance-project-animation-051022) |
| A brand story without product shots | Chipotle, "Back to the Start" (2011; CAA; dir. Johnny Kelly, Nexus Studios) | A stop-motion farm story set to Willie Nelson's cover of Coldplay's "The Scientist". |

## Sound

| Problem | Case | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Scoring a technical subject without cliché | Antfood, score for Google's "Hacking Google" series | Tactile acoustic instruments rather than the dark synthetic textures of cyber tropes, alongside orchestral and synth material. [Antfood](https://www.antfood.com/work/hacking-google-score) |
| Effects only, no music | Kia EV6, "Silence Is Powerful" (2023; David&Goliath / Innocean) | By its sound designer's account, picture and sound design only, with no voiceover or music. Watch it to confirm before citing. |
| An ending that refuses triumph | UNHCR, "The Journey" (Don't Panic; sound by String & Tins) | "The music is not triumphant at the end of the film"; the sound resists telling the listener how to feel. [D&AD](https://www.dandad.org/work/annual-2022/behind-the-work/composing-sound-design-to-be-the-hero) |
| Music made from the product's own sounds | Apple, "Start Up" (2021 event opener; A.G. Cook) | A song built from the sounds of Apple devices, opening on a startup chime. |
| Detailed sound on simple visuals | David Kamp (Studiokamp) | Chooses which visual elements need a sound, and uses sound to say what minimal visuals do not. [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/david-kamp-interview/) |
| Texture-first scoring for a creative tool | Procreate Dreams launch film (2023; dir. Alex Grigg; music by Skillbard) | Music built up from rich, tactile samples. Credits for this launch differ between sources; this is the Motion Awards set. |
