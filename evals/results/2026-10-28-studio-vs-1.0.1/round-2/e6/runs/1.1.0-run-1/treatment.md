# Treatment — proposed, not approved

Change: a viewer sees one receipt become a set of item-level shares and understands what Pocketfern's new feature is. Boundary: the pictures cannot establish the app's exact workflow until real capture is supplied.

Three directions to compare as rough motion sketches:

1. **The receipt, resolved** (product in use; recommended). Open on a receipt with several distinct items. Show a real Pocketfern interaction assigning items, then hold on its real result. The peak is the transition from one list to individual shares. Close with Pocketfern, “Split by item”, availability and free status. Look: restrained product-led typography; sound: light rhythm and tactile accents. Risk: unverified workflow and result.
2. **One receipt, different people** (relational analogy). A paper receipt is visually separated into item strips beside simple person markers, then the actual app supplies the proof. The peak is the side-by-side relation. Look: tactile paper and clean type; sound: soft paper texture. Risk: strips could imply physical separation or automatic allocation; label the illustration and avoid showing unsupported behavior.
3. **The question changes** (reframe). Open “One receipt.” Then “Different items.” Reveal the feature through real capture, followed by the 50-item limit. Look: typography anchored to the product; sound: a measured build. Risk: too abstract without the demonstration.

Provisional governing operation: a single receipt becomes understandable at item level. Stable: receipt identity and item names. Change: the relationship between each item and its participant, only as shown by verified capture. Do not imply calculation, speed or settlement. The analogy stops at illustration.

Tests: operation is intelligible in a single receipt-to-item relationship, but must be tested in rough motion; truth depends on real capture; decoding must be checked with a new viewer; specificity comes from the actual Pocketfern interface and named feature, not a generic receipt; muted comprehension requires visible cause, action and result and a named brand. These are hypotheses, not viewer results. If the feature failed, an actual capture would fail to show the item-level split and its resulting state. Do not substitute invented success screens.
